A LITTLE HELP

Room for your questions.

Frequently asked questions about the public beta, preparation and the conversations behind Room to Say.

Room to Say is an appointment companion in public beta, inspired by the experience of living with a long-term condition.

App support

For questions or help with Room to Say, email support@roomtosay.org. Please avoid including sensitive health information in your first message. This address is not an urgent-care service.

Frequently asked questions

What are you building?

Room to Say is an iPhone appointment-preparation app in public beta. Keep dated notes and questions, organise them around your care team, and prepare a brief or PDF to bring to a healthcare conversation.

Why did you start?

My parents taught me to prepare questions and notes after I was diagnosed with ankylosing spondylitis at seventeen. I want to offer a little help to people learning that habit for themselves. — Max, founder

Is it only for arthritis?

Arthritis is where my own story begins. People with different long-term conditions and appointments may also find preparation useful. The beta will help us understand how well the design fits different experiences.

Is it for parents and carers?

Parents’ and carers’ perspectives are part of the intended direction. We’ll explain which caring workflows the beta supports before inviting people to rely on them.

Does it give medical advice?

No. Its purpose is preparation and communication. Diagnosis and treatment decisions belong in conversations with qualified healthcare professionals.

Is it available, and what does it cost?

The public beta is now available through TestFlight. Check the listing for current build information and device requirements. The app is still in development; any future pricing will be announced separately.

Does it use AI?

AI summary generation is disabled in the public beta while it is being reworked. You can prepare a brief manually or create a PDF of your selected notes without AI. On-device AI remains a development track, not a currently available beta feature.

Is Room to Say a charity?

Room to Say is an independent project inspired by lived experience. We are not presenting it as a registered charity.

Can I use the preparation prompts now?

Yes. The homepage has three simple prompts you can tick or print. The ticks are temporary and are not saved or sent by this website.

Questions about trust

I already use a notebook. Why would I need this?

A notebook may work well for you. Room to Say is exploring whether a little structure around appointments is useful to some people. Your existing approach can help us understand what the app needs to do well.

Will this be another thing to manage?

Preparation should feel approachable. We want beta feedback on where the app helps and where it creates extra work, so we can understand what needs to change.

Will my doctor have to install it?

No. You can read your prepared brief on your own screen or preview and share a PDF of your brief or selected notes. Your doctor does not need an account or the app.

Will sponsors see my information?

The proposed sponsorship approach does not offer sponsors access to identifiable health information. No organisation is presented here as an existing sponsor. Contacting us by email or sending TestFlight feedback is separate from storing notes in the app; share only what you intend to send.

Will AI change what I said?

AI summary generation is disabled in the public beta. The notes document includes selected entries in your own words; a manual brief uses the wording you prepare. Preview any document before sharing it. Future AI work must be tested before it is offered to testers.

Are you speaking for all patients?

No. My own experience is why I started. Other people’s experiences need to help shape the project, including where their needs differ from mine. — Max, founder

Is this replacing clinical care?

No. It is being built to support preparation for conversations with healthcare professionals. It is not a treatment service or an urgent-care route. This website is not monitored for urgent requests; contact your healthcare team or local emergency services as appropriate.

Using the public beta

The beta is still being tested and improved. Check TestFlight for your available build and its What to Test notes.

Will I need an account or an internet connection?

The current beta works locally and offline without a Room to Say account or sign-in. iCloud sync is disabled. Downloading or updating through TestFlight and sharing through an online service require connectivity.

Will my notes move to a new phone?

There is no automatic iCloud sync in the public beta. Use Profile → Back up and restore to export an encrypted backup before changing phones or deleting the app. Keep the backup file and its passphrase safe: both are needed to restore. Future iCloud continuity is being developed and tested; do not rely on it for recovery.

Can I lock the app?

An optional app lock is available in Profile → Privacy, using Face ID or Touch ID with device-passcode fallback. Widget and app-lock behaviour on physical devices is still part of beta testing; do not treat an app lock as a guarantee of complete privacy.

Can I organise notes by condition and doctor?

Yes. Notes can be unassigned or linked to a doctor and an appointment, with optional health-condition labels you enter yourself. Filters help you find relevant notes. When preparing a brief, you can explicitly include a note from elsewhere without moving the original.

Can I revisit past appointments?

Yes. Your health story brings notes, check-ins and completed visits into a dated history. Doctor spaces also keep relevant appointment history. Mark a visit complete yourself; a date passing does not mean you attended.

Will AI summaries work on every iPhone?

AI summary generation is disabled in the public beta. Manual briefs and notes documents do not require AI. The core app targets iPhone with iOS 17 or later; check the current TestFlight listing for compatibility. Experimental on-device AI requires additional device, software and language support.

Which languages are available?

The beta offers English (US, UK, Australia and Canada), Polish, German, French, Spanish, Arabic and Japanese. Non-English translations are machine-generated and have not yet been reviewed by speakers. Your own notes are not translated. Please report unclear wording or layout problems through TestFlight. Some system permission prompts currently remain in English.

Can I scan or import medical letters?

Letter scanning and photo/PDF import are implemented in the uploaded 0.2.2 update, but availability to public testers has not yet been confirmed. Check TestFlight before relying on these features. The update lets you review recognised text, dates and professional links before saving; recognition can be wrong and handwriting is not guaranteed. Physical-iPhone scanning checks remain outstanding.

What should I know before updating?

Export an encrypted backup first and keep its passphrase safe. A backup created in 0.2.2 cannot be restored into 0.2.1. In 0.2.2, saved letters and originals are included in encrypted backups; unfinished letter imports remain on that iPhone and are not included.
