A LITTLE HELP

Room for your questions.

Frequently asked questions about Room to Say, preparing for appointments and keeping your records on your iPhone.

Room to Say is an appointment companion for iPhone, inspired by the experience of living with a long-term condition. These answers describe the intended version 1.0 release, currently in preparation, which keeps your records on your iPhone and does not sync through iCloud.

App support

For questions or help with Room to Say, email support@roomtosay.org. Please avoid including sensitive health information in your first message. This address is not an urgent-care service and is not monitored for urgent requests.

First steps in V1.0

The current V1.0 candidate includes the features below. Public App Store availability has not yet been confirmed; TestFlight builds may differ.

  1. Home: add a quick note, record how you have been, or open an appointment to prepare.
  2. Care team: add a professional and appointments. Use Notes to find your entries and filters; Profile contains your conditions, medications and settings.
  3. Letters: choose Scan or import a letter. Review OCR text, dates, professional links and medication suggestions before saving. Recognition can be wrong; suggestions do not replace medical advice. Saved letters retain their originals.
  4. Prepare: select notes and questions for a visit. Preview a notes document or brief PDF and deliberately choose Share.

Back up and move records

Open Profile → Back up and move records. Choose Back up now to export an encrypted backup; save it somewhere you can access away from this phone. Keep its passphrase safe: it cannot be recovered. Use Restore from a backup to select the file and enter its passphrase. Review the replacement confirmation and back up destination records first.

To move directly, choose Transfer to another device on the sending iPhone and the receive option on the other. Keep both nearby, allow Local Network access when requested, scan or enter the receiving phone’s one-time code, check that both devices show the same short code, and confirm the destination replacement. Camera scanning is optional; use the provided code-entry option if needed. Keep both screens open until completion.

Transfer replaces destination records rather than merging them; the source stays intact. It needs both devices and does not continuously sync them or recover an already lost phone. Unsaved note drafts, an unfinished letter review, app-lock setting, notification permission, widget state and backup-reminder status are not transferred. Unsaved note drafts and unfinished letter reviews are also excluded from encrypted backups.

Before deleting the app or changing phones, export an encrypted backup and check that you can find both the file and its passphrase. V1.0 has no automatic iCloud sync. The app requests exclusion of care records from ordinary device backups; do not rely on automatic iOS backup or migration to preserve them.

Reporting a problem

Email support@roomtosay.org with the app version/build, iPhone model, iOS version and steps to reproduce. Describe what you expected and what happened. Use made-up examples and remove names, health details and pairing codes from screenshots. Do not attach a medical letter, backup file or backup passphrase.

Older PDFs

If an earlier version produced a multi-page PDF with pages after the first upside down, regenerate the document with the fixed V1.0 version. Updating the app does not repair files already exported or sent.

Frequently asked questions

What are you building?

Room to Say is an iPhone appointment-preparation app. Keep dated notes and questions, organise them around your care team, and prepare a brief or PDF to bring to a healthcare conversation.

Why did you start?

My parents taught me to prepare questions and notes after I was diagnosed with ankylosing spondylitis at seventeen. I want to offer a little help to people learning that habit for themselves. — Max, founder

Is it only for arthritis?

Arthritis is where my own story begins. People with different long-term conditions and appointments may also find preparation useful. Feedback from different experiences helps us understand how well the design fits.

Is it for parents and carers?

You can record information for your own care or for someone you support, where you are entitled to do so. There is one profile, with no separate family or child profiles. Review whose information a document contains before sharing.

Does it give medical advice?

No. Its purpose is preparation and communication. Diagnosis and treatment decisions belong in conversations with qualified healthcare professionals.

Is it available, and what does it cost?

Version 1.0 is being prepared for release on the App Store. Until it is released, testing versions are available to testers through TestFlight, and some testing versions differ from 1.0 (see the iCloud question below). Pricing will be shown on the App Store listing. The app needs an iPhone with iOS 17 or later.

Does it use AI?

No. Version 1.0 does not use AI to write, summarise or change your notes. You prepare a brief yourself, or create a PDF of your selected notes in your own words. Room to Say does not give medical advice.

Is Room to Say a charity?

Room to Say is an independent project inspired by lived experience. We are not presenting it as a registered charity.

Can I use the preparation prompts now?

Yes. The homepage has three simple prompts you can tick or print. The ticks are temporary and are not saved or sent by this website.

Questions about trust

I already use a notebook. Why would I need this?

A notebook may work well for you. Room to Say is exploring whether a little structure around appointments is useful to some people. Your existing approach can help us understand what the app needs to do well.

Will this be another thing to manage?

Preparation should feel approachable. We want feedback on where the app helps and where it creates extra work, so we can understand what needs to change.

Will my doctor have to install it?

No. You can read your prepared brief on your own screen or preview and share a PDF of your brief or selected notes. Your doctor does not need an account or the app.

Will sponsors see my information?

The proposed sponsorship approach does not offer sponsors access to identifiable health information. No organisation is presented here as an existing sponsor. Contacting us by email or sending TestFlight feedback is separate from storing notes in the app; share only what you intend to send.

Will AI change what I said?

No. Version 1.0 has no AI generation. The notes document includes selected entries in your own words; a brief uses the wording you prepare. Preview any document before sharing it.

Are you speaking for all patients?

No. My own experience is why I started. Other people’s experiences need to help shape the project, including where their needs differ from mine. — Max, founder

Is this replacing clinical care?

No. It is being built to support preparation for conversations with healthcare professionals. It is not a treatment service or an urgent-care route. This website is not monitored for urgent requests; contact your healthcare team or local emergency services as appropriate.

Using Room to Say 1.0

If you are using a TestFlight testing version, check TestFlight for your build and its What to Test notes.

Will I need an account or an internet connection?

No Room to Say account is needed. Core records and document preparation work locally. V1.0 has no automatic iCloud sync. Nearby transfer uses a local network or peer connection; downloading updates and destinations you choose for sharing may need internet access.

Will my notes move to a new phone?

Use an encrypted backup and restore, or deliberately pair two nearby iPhones for a one-time transfer. Both phones are needed for nearby transfer. The receiving records are replaced, not merged; back them up first. The sender keeps its records. Neither method is continuous sync.

Can I lock the app?

Yes. An optional app lock is in Profile → Privacy and your data, using Face ID, Touch ID or your device passcode. It is one layer of protection, not a guarantee of complete privacy.

Can I organise notes by condition and doctor?

Yes. Notes can be unassigned or linked to a doctor and an appointment, with optional health-condition labels you enter yourself. Filters help you find relevant notes. When preparing a brief, you can explicitly include a note from elsewhere without moving the original.

Can I revisit past appointments?

Yes. Your health story brings notes, check-ins and completed visits into a dated history. Doctor spaces also keep relevant appointment history. Mark a visit complete yourself; a date passing does not mean you attended.

Does it work on every iPhone?

Version 1.0 needs an iPhone with iOS 17 or later. Briefs and notes documents are prepared without AI.

Which languages are available?

English (US, UK, Australia and Canada), Polish, German, French, Spanish, Arabic and Japanese. Your own notes, names and conditions are never translated.

Can I scan or import medical letters?

Yes. Scan a letter or select photos/PDFs, review the extracted text and proposed details, then save it with its original. OCR runs on the device and can make mistakes. Check dates, links and medication suggestions before accepting them.

I used a TestFlight version with iCloud sync. What should I know?

Some testing versions could sync optionally through iCloud. Version 1.0 cannot, so it cannot download anything that was still waiting in iCloud. Your records already on the iPhone are kept when you update. Before updating, open your current version, check that Profile → Keep your history with iCloud says it is up to date and that nothing is still arriving, and export an encrypted backup. If the app shows a note after updating that some files had not reached the iPhone, or you are unsure, email support@roomtosay.org before deleting the app or its data.
