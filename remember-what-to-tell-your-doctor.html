A LITTLE HELP BEFORE YOUR VISIT

Forget what to tell your doctor? A simple way to prepare

By Max, founder of Room to Say · 28 October 2026

You may remember something important on the way home from an appointment. Having a note to refer to can take some pressure off remembering everything in the room.

Give each thought a place

Keep a running list in a notebook or a place on your phone that you can find easily. Add questions as they occur to you rather than relying on the morning of the appointment.

A note does not have to be polished. Try this format:

“Date: ___. What I noticed: ___. What it affected: ___. What I want to ask: ___.”

Before the visit, make a short list

Review what you’ve recorded and choose the two or three questions you most want to discuss. Keep the longer notes available for detail. Put the short list somewhere you can open without searching.

Use the list during the conversation

Tell your clinician that you have written down your concerns. You can read from the list and tick off points as they are discussed. If you want someone with you, ask a friend or family member to help take notes.

Make space for the answer, too

Write down what happens next. If you are unsure you understood, ask for the explanation again or check the plan in your own words.

If you remember something afterwards

Use the contact route your clinic gives you to ask how to pass on an additional question. This website and its contact inboxes are not a route for contacting your clinician.

You can use these prompts today with your own notebook or phone. Room to Say is still in development; you don’t need the app to begin preparing.

A note about this guide

General preparation and communication information, written from a patient perspective. This is not medical advice or a clinically reviewed resource. Follow your healthcare team’s instructions for your own visit.
