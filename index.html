A LITTLE PREPARATION. A LITTLE MORE SPACE.

You know your everyday.
Bring it into the room.

You might wait months for an appointment. When it arrives, remembering everything at once can be hard. Our aim is to make preparation a habit you can build on, one thought at a time.

PLANNED FOR VERSION 1.0

Your notes.
Your way of preparing.

These are our plans for the first iPhone release, not features confirmed as available. We’ll verify them through development and beta testing.

Capture life between visits.

Quick dated notes, including earlier dates when you need them, with room for more detail. Optional check-ins, local reminders, and a quick feeling widget are planned too.

Keep your care connected.

Separate spaces for your doctors, with initials by default and an optional photo you provide. Add your own condition tags to notes, independently of the doctor or appointment they belong to.

Choose what to bring.

Select relevant notes for an appointment brief, including notes from elsewhere in your care history, without moving or copying the originals.

A draft you can make your own.

On-device AI summary drafts are planned for supported devices, software, and languages, with references to your notes. Edit and review before sharing, or prepare a brief manually when AI is unavailable.

Look back on your conversations.

Find attended appointments across your care team or for one doctor, alongside briefs, questions, and saved answers. A cancelled appointment, or one whose date has passed, won’t be treated as an attended visit.

Privacy, with choices.

Local-first use without a Room to Say account, optional private iCloud sync, and optional Face ID or Touch ID app lock with device-passcode fallback are planned. Read our privacy plans.

WHY ROOM TO SAY EXISTS

A habit my
parents taught me.
A little help
for someone else.

I was diagnosed with ankylosing spondylitis when I was seventeen.

I was still at school, trying to understand what was happening, and spending a lot of time going to and from hospital appointments.

My parents taught me to arrive with a list of questions and notes about how I’d been feeling. With appointments often lasting just ten or fifteen minutes, and potentially months until the next one, they wanted me to make the most of that time.

It became a habit. Over the years, I’ve learned how to explain what’s been happening, be honest about how I feel, and ask the questions that matter to me. I believe that preparation has helped shape my care and the relationship I have with my doctor.

Alongside my own experience as a patient, my patient advocacy work has included working with EULAR, the pharmaceutical company UCB, and the National Ankylosing Spondylitis Society (NASS, now the National Axial Spondyloarthritis Society). I’ve also been part of Arthritis Research’s young persons’ panel.

But it’s a skill I was taught. If you’re young, newly diagnosed, or caring for a child, you may be working it out for the first time.

That’s why I’m building Room to Say: to help others prepare for those conversations, and help healthcare professionals understand the person in front of them.

M
MaxFounder, Room to Say

GOOD TO KNOW

A little room
for your questions.

About the idea, who it’s for, and what comes next.

Is Room to Say only for arthritis?

Arthritis is where Max’s story begins. Preparation can be useful across different conditions and appointments. The beta will help us understand how well the design fits different experiences.

Does it give medical advice?

No. Room to Say is being built to support preparation and communication with your healthcare team. It does not replace clinical care.

When can I try it?

A public beta is planned. We’ll share dates, supported devices, participation details, and pricing once confirmed.

Will my doctor have to install it?

We plan to let you review preparation on your own screen or export a printable summary, without a clinician account. We’ll confirm which sharing options are available before the beta opens.
