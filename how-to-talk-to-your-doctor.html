A LITTLE HELP BEFORE YOUR VISIT

How to talk to your doctor and explain what matters

By Max, founder of Room to Say · 28 October 2026

If you’re wondering how to talk to your doctor, start with what brought you to the appointment and what you most want to understand. You don’t need medical language or a perfect explanation.

Before you go: choose your priorities

Write down your two or three most important questions. Add what you’ve noticed, when it started, and how it affects everyday life. A specific example can be easier to explain than trying to describe everything at once.

A way to start the conversation

“The main thing I want to talk about today is ___. It started ___. It’s affecting ___. I’d like to understand ___.”

This is a prompt you can adapt, not a script you have to follow. It is okay to read your notes aloud or say that you’re nervous.

What if I don’t feel heard or understood?

Try returning to the concern you want to discuss: “I’m still worried about ___. Could we talk about that before we finish?” You can also ask the clinician to explain their thinking or use different words. Preparation cannot guarantee how a conversation will go.

Check what happens next

Before leaving, check what the plan is, when you should expect results or follow-up, and who to contact with further questions. You can repeat the plan in your own words to check your understanding.

Do I need to be a “better patient”?

You deserve respectful care even when you’re anxious, forget something, or find it hard to explain. Preparation is a tool to support you, not a test. If you need an interpreter or communication support, ask the clinic in advance.

A note about this guide

General preparation and communication information, written from a patient perspective. This is not medical advice or a clinically reviewed resource. Follow your healthcare team’s instructions for your own visit.
